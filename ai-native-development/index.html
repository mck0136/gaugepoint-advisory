01 / The SaaS era

A pretty good deal.
With a catch.

For two decades, SaaS made sophisticated software accessible without the cost and complexity of building it all yourself.

Subscriptions brought enterprise applications within reach. Vendors managed infrastructure and updates. Businesses could get moving without assembling a development team or investing in their own technology stack.

That was a real shift, and it remains the right answer for plenty of needs. But as businesses added more tools, the tradeoffs compounded: recurring subscriptions, vendor dependency, generic workflows, and roadmaps set somewhere else.

Sometimes the work itself had to bend around the software. Sometimes the software did most of what was needed, but not the part that made the business different.

02 / The new economics

What happens when
building becomes viable?

The answer isn’t “build everything.”
It’s “choose with better options.”

AI-assisted development is changing the effort involved in creating and adapting software. That puts a new option on the table for more businesses.

Buy what is proven and works well. Build where your process is distinctive and the case is sound. Enhance what you already own when a focused change will do the job.

Custom software is not automatically cheaper. Compare the whole picture: build and change costs, subscriptions, hosting, integration, security, and ongoing maintenance. SaaS isn’t disappearing; the balance of a software portfolio may change.

For a broader perspective on changing SaaS and custom-software choices: Deloitte, “SaaS isn’t disappearing, but its role is changing”
